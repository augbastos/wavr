Privacy

Plain language, not legalese. Two separate things to explain: what the Wavr product does with the data it senses, and what this website does with your visit. Short version: the product keeps your data on your own hardware, and the website collects nothing at all.

The product: your home stays yours

Runs where you put it

Wavr runs on hardware you own: a laptop, a mini PC, or a Raspberry Pi. There is no Wavr cloud service; nothing is uploaded anywhere by default.

Loopback by default

Out of the box, Wavr only answers requests from the same machine it's running on. It doesn't even open a network port other devices could reach until you deliberately turn that on.

Cameras boot off

If you connect a camera, it starts disabled. When you turn it on, video frames are processed in memory to detect a person's presence and are never written to disk and never sent anywhere. Only "someone is in this room" is kept, never the picture.

Only the conclusion is stored

What's saved is occupancy, a confidence score, and a timestamp, per room. Not frames, not raw sensor coordinates, not your credentials.

Every door out is a switch you flip

Letting a second device see your dashboard, publishing state to Home Assistant, letting an AI agent read presence over MCP, having a narrator summarize your day: each is its own opt-in toggle, and each starts OFF.

Even the AI summary can stay local

If you turn on the natural-language narrator, you can point it at a local model running on your own machine, and that step never leaves your network either. Cloud providers are opt-in, not the default.

The website: nothing collected, nothing sent

This site is static files. No account, no cookies, no analytics, no third-party embeds, no fonts or scripts loaded from anywhere but this same site.

The one page that reads anything from your browser is Install, to guess which platform to show you first. Here is the complete list of what it reads and what it does with it:

What it readsWhyWhere it goes
navigator.userAgentData (if your browser exposes it), including a high-entropy read of platform, architecture and mobile flags To guess Windows, macOS, Linux, Android or iOS, and whether you're on a phone Nowhere. Read and used to pick which text to show, then discarded.
navigator.userAgent (fallback when the above isn't available) Same guess, for browsers without the newer API Nowhere. Never leaves your device.
A same-origin fetch of assets/releases.json, a file this site ships To check whether a download link exists for your platform yet Stays entirely within this site; it's a request to itself, not a third party.

That's the entire list. No canvas fingerprinting, no font enumeration, no battery/sensor APIs, no cookies or local-storage writes, no request to any analytics, advertising, or tracking service. Nothing about your visit is recorded by this site, because there is no server-side code here to record it: it's files, served as-is.

The guess is also explicitly labelled as a guess on the Install page itself: an iPad in desktop mode, for instance, can look identical to a Mac to any website. That's a known limitation of browser-based detection everywhere, not something specific to Wavr, and it's why the page never blocks you from picking a different platform yourself.

Questions

If something here needs a closer look, or a change to the fixed wording the product itself shows around cameras and data leaving your network, open an issue on GitHub.

Wavr is free and open source, AGPL-3.0-or-later. Local-only by design: no account, no cloud, no telemetry, on this site or in the product.
